# When the problem is a choice

Load this when the mode is "choose between options". `problemType.type` is "choose between options", and the same sections do slightly different work.

Example: "should we move weekend orders to a new ordering app?". The title is the question. `theirTheory` is the preference the question implies ("Has the current form become the problem?"), or omitted when it implies none. `gap` states the choice and, if a symptom sits behind it (missing orders), the symptom; `isItReal`, `changes`, `impact` and `detection` are about that symptom, and are omitted when there is none. The parts are the dimensions the choice turns on (cost per order, what each option can and cannot do, what it takes to switch). The IS / IS NOT columns are the two options, and the "distinctive" column says what a difference on that dimension means for the choice. The explanations are "A wins if..." conditions: `predicts` is what the records show if the condition holds, `explains` / `doesNotExplain` may be omitted, and two conditions may sit on the same dimension as long as their `ruleOut` evidence differs. The summary picks an option conditionally when the evidence cannot yet decide ("stay with A unless the missing orders turn out to be the form's doing"), and names the condition that decides; for a choice, line 1 may run to two sentences.
