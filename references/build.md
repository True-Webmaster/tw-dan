# Build: something to make

Load this with [core.md](core.md) when the mode is "plan or build something" (a plan, a system, a campaign, a process: "plan a referral programme", "set up reporting for this client"). core.md gives every section's shape and rules; this file says what each section means for a build, and governs where the two differ. `problemType.type` is "build something".

- **Title and their theory.** `title` names the thing to build and for whom ("Monthly reporting for a landscaping client"). `theirTheory` is the approach the person already has in mind, as a question ("Is a referral card at the counter the way to get more patients from patients?"). If they named none, the most common approach in that trade stands in, labelled as an assumption; when the request is generic ("monthly reporting"), list its smallest form and say the fuller version folds in.
- **The gap** is the goal: the outcome, for whom, by when, in the person's words. `impact` is what the outcome is worth. `detection` is how you will know it worked.
- **Is it worth it?** `isItReal` asks whether the outcome is worth the build: `outside normal` = clearly yes, `inside normal` = marginal, `can't tell yet` = size of the prize unknown (the report relabels them). `why` names what the prize was compared against.
- **What exists today:** `changes` holds what the build starts from or must fit: tools, data, people, past attempts.
- **The three vantages** for the walkthrough (team path: one helper each; solo path: take them in turn), with their tags:
  - `user`, **the first user.** "Be the first person who uses the thing once it exists, from the moment they meet it to the moment it has done its job for them. Where would they stall, misunderstand, or get the result some other way?"
  - `runs`, **the person who runs it.** "Be the person who has to operate, feed or maintain it every week after launch. What has to exist, be entered, be checked or be fixed for it to keep working, and what would quietly let it rot?"
  - `sceptic`, **the sceptic.** "Assume it should not be built. What already does this job, what would make it unnecessary, and what is the simplest thing that gets most of the outcome?"
- **The parts** are the building blocks and stages that together produce the outcome, and `dependsOn` is build order. One block is always the measurement: how the result gets counted. The coverage test names an outcome no block produces and one that two blocks produce. `danger: true` marks a block that is cheap to skip and costly if skipped (consent, data loss, a legal step).
- **IS / IS NOT** compares the thing to be built with the closest thing that already works (the person's past build, a competitor's, the manual version; for a first-ever build, how the job happens informally today); the `distinction` column says what a difference on that dimension would mean for the design. Skip it with a reason when there is nothing to compare.
- **The explanations** are competing approaches ("A gets us there if..."). Include the person's own approach and at least one simpler one, and, in place of diagnose.md's "the counting changed", "the goal is already met another way" (the outcome exists and nobody is measuring it), which rules out on records the person already holds (past requests, payments, promises), not on asking. `predicts` is what you would see at the first checkpoint if the approach is right; `ruleOut` is what would show it is wrong; `cheapestCheck` is the smallest prototype or test that tells.
- **Abstract, reuse, streams, rubric, tests.** `measure` is the number people will watch, `goal` the outcome; "matters now" is the critical path, "set aside" the nice-to-haves. Reuse is central: name what exists to call before building anything. Streams are build stages in waves; a prototype, a draft or a pilot is a `check`, not a fix (a pilot that customers see is a `check` with door `one-way`); one-way doors are a public launch, spend, a migration, anything customers see. Rubric criteria are acceptance criteria for blocks, still three on a first run; when a `danger` block is a legal, consent or platform-policy step, its acceptance criterion comes first, sourced to the law or the platform's own rule, the one exception to core.md's rule that exposures go in the blind spots (the detail of the exposure still goes there). Tests are one per stage, with the signal the one number that says the build worked.
- **Summary and next action.** Line 1 is the recommended shape of the build (or "two approaches cannot yet be separated"), line 2 the first stage, line 3 what the shape depends on. The next action is the smallest piece to build or test today (a prototype, a draft, a test record), never the whole thing; its `rulesOut` names which approaches that piece tells apart, or says it tells none apart yet.
- **Helper brief (team path).** In the brief in team.md, replace "explanations for the facts" with "approaches that would get to the goal, each with what you would see at the first checkpoint if it is right, what would show it is wrong, and the smallest prototype that tells", and use the build variant of its step 5.
