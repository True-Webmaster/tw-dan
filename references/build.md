# When the task is to build something

Load this when the mode is "plan or build something" (a plan, a system, a campaign, a process: "plan a referral programme", "set up reporting for this client"). `problemType.type` is "build something". Every section keeps its shape but changes its question:

- `gap` is the goal: the outcome, for whom, by when, in the person's words. `isItReal` becomes "is it worth it": is the outcome worth the build (`outside normal` = clearly yes, `inside normal` = marginal, `can't tell yet` = size of the prize unknown; the report relabels them). `impact` is what the outcome is worth; `detection` is how you will know it worked. `changes` becomes what exists today that the build starts from or must fit (tools, data, people, past attempts).
- `theirTheory` is the approach the person already has in mind, as a question ("Is a referral card at the counter the way to get more patients from patients?"). If they named none, the most common approach in that trade stands in, labelled as an assumption; when the request is generic ("monthly reporting"), list its smallest form and say the fuller version folds in.
- The walkthrough's vantages become the three listed at the end of this file (solo path: take them in turn, tagging entries `user`, `runs`, `sceptic`).
- The parts are the building blocks and stages that together produce the outcome, and `dependsOn` is build order. One block is always the measurement: how the result gets counted. The coverage test names an outcome no block produces and one that two blocks produce.
- IS / IS NOT compares the thing to be built with the closest thing that already works (the person's past build, a competitor's, the manual version; for a first-ever build, how the job happens informally today); the "distinctive" column says what a difference on that dimension would mean for the design. Skip it with a reason when there is nothing to compare.
- The explanations become competing approaches ("A gets us there if..."): `predicts` is what you would see at the first checkpoint if the approach is right, `ruleOut` is what would show it is wrong, `cheapestCheck` is the smallest prototype or test that tells. Include the person's own approach and at least one simpler one. The "counting changed" explanation is replaced by "the goal is already met another way" (the outcome exists and nobody is measuring it); it rules out on records the person already holds (past requests, payments, promises), not on asking.
- `danger: true` on a part (fill.py and the report accept it there as well as on an explanation) marks a block that is cheap to skip and costly if skipped (consent, data loss, a legal step); the report draws it with a red border and a "don't skip" tag. When such a block is a legal or consent step, its acceptance criterion belongs in the rubric, sourced to the rule, the one exception to method.md's rule that exposures go in the blind spots; the detail of the exposure still goes there.
- Abstract: `measure` is the number people will watch, `goal` the outcome; "matters now" is the critical path, "set aside" the nice-to-haves. Reuse is central: name what exists to call before building anything. Streams are build stages in waves; a prototype, a draft or a pilot is a `check`, not a fix (a pilot that customers see is a `check` with door `one-way`); one-way doors are a public launch, spend, a migration, anything customers see. Rubric criteria are acceptance criteria for blocks, still three on a first run, the legal or consent block's first. Tests are one per stage, with the signal the one number that says the build worked.
- `nextAction` is the smallest piece to build or test today (a prototype, a draft, a test record), never the whole thing. Summary line 1 is the recommended shape of the build (or "two approaches cannot yet be separated"), line 2 the first stage, line 3 what the shape depends on.

## The three helper vantages for a build (team path)

- **The first user.** "Be the first person who uses the thing once it exists, from the moment they meet it to the moment it has done its job for them. Where would they stall, misunderstand, or get the result some other way?"
- **The person who runs it.** "Be the person who has to operate, feed or maintain it every week after launch. What has to exist, be entered, be checked or be fixed for it to keep working, and what would quietly let it rot?"
- **The sceptic.** "Assume it should not be built. What already does this job, what would make it unnecessary, and what is the simplest thing that gets most of the outcome?"

In the helper brief, replace "explanations for the facts" with "approaches that would get to the goal, each with what you would see at the first checkpoint if it is right, what would show it is wrong, and the smallest prototype that tells", and use the build variant of step 5. Tag kept walkthrough entries `user`, `runs`, `sceptic`.
