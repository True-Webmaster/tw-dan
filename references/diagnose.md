# Diagnose: something is wrong

Load this with [core.md](core.md) when the mode is "find out what is going on". core.md gives every section's shape and rules; this file says what each section means when something has gone wrong, and governs where the two differ.

- **Title and their theory.** `title` names the symptom ("Why weekend pre-orders are missing at pickup"), never the suspected cause. `theirTheory`: when the problem statement names a cause ("because of the new order form"), the neutral question is "Did the new order form reduce weekend orders?"; when the blame is general ("the Saturday special isn't working", "the new hire isn't performing"), ask about the mechanism it implies ("Are customers seeing the special before they order?"); a doubt implied without a cause ("the dashboard says X but Y happened") counts too.
- **The gap** is what is observed versus what is expected, with the numbers, unit and period if the person gave any. `impact` is what the gap costs, or "unknown". `detection` is how and when it was noticed; a problem noticed three months late is itself a finding.
- **Is it real?** Sales, visits and enquiries fluctuate. `outside normal`: bigger than the usual swings (reported figures are enough when the swing is far beyond anything seasonal; say so in `why`). `inside normal`: could be ordinary variation, seasonality, or a bounce-back from an unusually good period. `can't tell yet`: no baseline seen and the size of the swing could be ordinary. `why` names the baseline to compare against (the same measure for the last 12 months). If the verdict is "inside normal", summary line 1 says so and the map becomes a watch plan.
- **What changed, and when:** anything that moved around the time the gap opened: the product, the price, the people, the tools, the way things are counted, a competitor, the season, a rule set by a platform the business depends on. Most problems are triggered by a change; this is Kepner-Tregoe's step after IS / IS NOT.
- **The three vantages** for the walkthrough (team path: one helper each; solo path: take them in turn), with their tags:
  - `customer`, **the customer's path.** "Follow one customer (or one order, one visit, one enquiry) from the first moment they appear to the last thing that happens to them. Where could they drop out, be turned away, be counted wrongly, or come back by another door?"
  - `records`, **the record-keeper.** "Follow how the number the person quoted gets made: who writes what down, in which tool, at which moment, under which rule. Where could the number move while nothing in the business moved?"
  - `sceptic`, **the sceptic.** "Assume nothing is wrong with the business. What else would produce exactly these facts: timing, season, a comparison that isn't like for like, something that changed elsewhere, an expectation that was never realistic?"
- **The parts** are the steps that together produce the result. Follow the path end to end; where the counts stop matching from one step to the next is usually where the problem is. The counting itself (the tool, the report, the rule that decides what gets counted) is a part when the person's numbers come through it. Each part's question is diagnostic: what would this step show if the problem lived here?
- **IS / IS NOT** compares the thing that goes wrong with the closest thing that goes right. If the person gave no working comparison, pick the closest one that almost certainly exists (the customers who stayed, the month before, the product that sells) and list it as an assumption. For each row write what the problem IS, what it IS NOT (though it could be), and, in `distinction`, what tells them apart. A number the person offers as the cause is one angle; find two independent indicators before leaning on it.
- **The explanations** are causes. Include at least one that goes against the most obvious answer, the person's own theory, and, whenever the problem is a number anyone reports, "the counting changed, not the business" (the tool that counts, the rule it uses, the period it compares) with its own check: send one test through the path end to end. `predicts` is what the records will show if this is the cause ("every missing order has an order time after the list was printed"); `ruleOut` is the evidence that would eliminate it; `cheapestCheck` is the least demanding check that separates it from the others.
- **Contributing factors** (`contributingFactors`): causes that only produce the failure together. Name them when the leading explanations need more than one condition to be true.
- **Summary and next action.** Line 1 is what is most likely going on, or that the evidence in hand cannot yet separate the front-runners. The next action is the check that rules out the most explanations at once; when it needs someone else's records, today's action is requesting them, plus the one check you can do meanwhile. Fixes wait for the checks.
- **Helper brief (team path).** The brief in team.md asks for "explanations for the facts" and the parts of the path; use it as written.
